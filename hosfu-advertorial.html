Solar chargers look great in marketing photos — blue skies, sunny beaches, hashtag-off-grid. Real emergencies don't wait for good weather. Hurricane? Cloud cover for days. Winter ice storm? Dark by 4pm. Power outage at midnight? Your solar panel is decorative.

In our tests, participants using solar-only options averaged just 2.1 hours of usable phone time over the 4-day blackout simulation — because our test fell during a cloudy stretch, which is exactly what happens in real disaster scenarios. It's not a bad product. It's just not a reliable emergency product.

Solar makes a great supplement to a primary emergency power source. It should not be the primary source itself.

Traditional Gas Generators
Portable Gasoline-Powered Generators
Champion, Westinghouse, Honda, etc.
D

✓ Pros

  • High power output — can run appliances
  • Proven technology, widely understood
  • Works in any weather (as long as you have fuel)

✗ Cons

  • Fuel runs out and disappears fast during real disasters
  • Extremely loud — can be heard from blocks away
  • Cannot be used indoors due to carbon monoxide risk
  • Hundreds to thousands of dollars upfront
  • Heavy, bulky — cannot go in a go-bag
  • Requires maintenance, oil changes, fuel stabilizer
  • Useless for camping, hiking, or vehicle emergencies
  • Fails exactly when fuel supply chains fail

Gas generators have their place — if you live in a hurricane zone and can store 50 gallons of treated fuel, they're a legitimate option for keeping your refrigerator running. But for the majority of Americans who need reliable emergency backup power they can actually have with them — in their car, at camp, at a hotel after an evacuation — gas generators are completely impractical.

The deeper problem: in real emergencies, gas stations go offline too. After major hurricanes and winter storms, lines at the few working gas stations stretch for miles. The people counting on fuel-dependent solutions are the ones who end up with nothing. Any power source that depends on a supply chain is a fragile power source.

Great for home standby with adequate fuel storage. Fails completely as a portable, always-ready emergency solution for most people.
